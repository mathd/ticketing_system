# Catalog's internal service-to-service routes are hand-mounted, outside the OpenAPI contract

**Date:** 2026-07-21 · **Ticket:** TKT-80 (PR #86)

## The fact

Catalog exposes its internal (`/internal/*`) service-to-service endpoints by **hand-mounting
them on the chi router** in `services/catalog/internal/api/server.go` — they are **not** declared
in `services/catalog/api/openapi.yaml`, and they are **not** generated by oapi-codegen. Adding a
new internal endpoint (e.g. TKT-80's batch seat pin/unpin) needs **no `openapi.yaml` change and
no `make generate`** — you register the route and write the handler.

This works because the ADR-028 response validator (`shared/go/contract/http.go`,
`responseValidated`) **skips any path `router.FindRoute` does not resolve** (`if routeErr == nil`
guards the whole validation block). An undeclared internal path therefore passes through
unvalidated, exactly like the pre-existing `/internal/ticket-types/{id}`,
`/internal/performances/{id}`, and `/internal/pools/{id}/offer-state` reads.

**Inventory is the opposite:** its request validator (`contract.RequestValidator`) wraps the whole
router and **rejects undeclared paths**, and inventory declares *every* route — public and internal
— in its `openapi.yaml`. So a new inventory endpoint (public or internal) **does** need an
`openapi.yaml` entry + `make generate`. The two services follow different conventions; match the
service you are editing.

## Why it's worth writing down

Both TKT-80 plan drafters (deepseek + gpt-5.6-sol) independently assumed the catalog pin endpoint
needed an OpenAPI + regen change (ADR-009 "contract-first"), over-scoping the diff. ADR-009's
contract-first rule governs **public** contracts served through the gateway; internal
service-to-service endpoints are a hand-mounted exception with existing precedent. Checking whether
the target service's existing `/internal/*` routes are in its `openapi.yaml` (catalog: no;
inventory: yes) tells you which path to take before you write the plan.
